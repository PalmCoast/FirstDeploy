What after-hours roofing calls look like

Roofing calls in Florida follow the weather. Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, and after a named storm the phones ring for days: missing shingles, lifted tiles, water stains, a tree limb through the soffit. Between storms, summer afternoon thunderstorms with wind-driven rain find the weak spots in flashing, vents and valleys. Leaks tend to show up during the storm or right after, which often means evenings and weekends. The drier months are when homeowners plan replacements and inspections, and they still call after hours, because that's when they're home to look at the ceiling.

Emergency rules we write with you

  • Active leak into the home. Water coming into a living space during or after a storm. The desk asks where, how much, and whether it's near light fixtures or outlets, and flags it under your rules.
  • Sagging or bulging ceiling. A ceiling bulging with water. The safety step you approve might be: keep everyone out of that room and away from what's beneath it.
  • Tree or limb on the roof. Structural damage. If anyone is hurt or power lines are down, the caller calls 911 first. Otherwise the desk takes the details and flags it urgent.
  • Exposed roof deck with rain coming. Shingles or tiles gone and more weather on the way. Flagged so you can decide on tarping. The desk never promises a tarp time.
  • Water near electrical. Water dripping from a light fixture or near the panel. The desk tells the caller to keep clear and flags it urgent.

You approve every word of these in the written plan before go-live. By default the desk never quotes prices, promises an arrival time, offers discounts or gives out your cell number, and every call opens with a recording notice.

A sample night call

  • Caller: "The storm tonight, and now there's a brown spot on the bedroom ceiling and it's dripping."
  • Desk: "I'm sorry. Let's get this to the owner. Is the ceiling sagging, or is it just the spot and the drip?" Caller: "Just the spot. I put a bucket under it."
  • Desk: "Good call. Is any water near a light fixture or outlet?" Caller: "No."
  • Desk: "Can I get your name and the best number to reach you?" (reads it back)
  • Desk: "And the address?" Caller: "The caller's address." Desk: "Do you know what kind of roof it is?" Caller: "Shingle, I think."
  • Desk: "Thank you. I'm sending this to the owner now marked as an active leak. I can't promise a time or say anything about insurance, but they'll have all of this when they call you back."

A minute later, you get one message with the caller's name, the callback number they confirmed, the address, the problem, and how urgent it is and why.

What the desk does

  • Answers as your shop. Your business name, your hours, your service area, and the work you do and don't take.
  • Asks what you'd ask. Name, callback number (read back to confirm), service address, and for roofing calls: where the water is coming in, whether it's still leaking, the ceiling's condition, the roof type if they know it (shingle, tile, metal or flat), and whether they've already started an insurance claim.
  • Sends you every call. The details reach you right away, so the job is waiting when you pick up your phone, not buried in voicemail.
  • Flags the urgent ones. Your emergency rules are written in, and real emergencies go to the top with the safety step you approved.
  • Keeps your number. Forward your line after hours or when you don't pick up. Nothing new to print on the truck.
  • Tested before go-live. We run practice calls with hard callers (angry, confused, panicked, robocalls) and fix what breaks before a customer hears it.
  • A person behind it. When you want a change, you call the operator who built it, not a ticket queue.
